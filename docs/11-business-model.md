# Business Model

Status: Strategic hypothesis; validate with users and unit economics

## Model

Aikya should use a freemium, usage-aware SaaS model with individual, professional, business, developer, and enterprise paths. The free experience proves real document value. Paid plans fund higher provider quality, larger workloads, collaboration, automation, and service guarantees.

The business must not depend on selling document data or using private content for training. Revenue comes from subscriptions, usage, enterprise contracts, and support.

## Customer segments

| Segment | Need | Value driver | Likely motion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Individual | occasional translation/OCR | easy, trustworthy completion | self-serve free |
| Professional | frequent documents/resumes | quality, speed, history, export | self-serve subscription |
| Team | shared terminology and spend | collaboration, governance | assisted/self-serve |
| Developer | embedded workflows | reliable API and observability | usage-based |
| Enterprise | control and assurance | SSO, audit, residency, SLA, private options | sales-led contract |

## Value and differentiation

- Format-aware translation rather than copy/paste text conversion.
- Transparent fidelity and review workflow.
- One pipeline across document, OCR, comparison, enhancement, and export.
- Privacy-aware provider choice and retention control.
- Organizational glossary and translation memory compounding over time.

## Revenue streams

1. Monthly/annual subscriptions with entitlements and included usage.
2. Usage overage or prepaid credits for high-volume document/API workloads.
3. Enterprise annual contracts for identity, governance, security, support, and private deployment.
4. Optional professional services for onboarding, terminology setup, integrations, and migration.

Do not sell lifetime “unlimited” access: provider, storage, compute, and support costs recur.

## Cost structure

Primary variable costs are translation characters, OCR/render compute, AI tokens, speech generation, storage, egress, malware scanning, email, and payment fees. Fixed costs include engineering, operations, security/compliance, support, legal, and design.

The unit-economics model must attribute cost to organization, feature, provider, format, pages/characters, and processing tier. Gross margin is monitored by plan and workload. Expensive features require quota reservation and concurrency controls.

## Go-to-market sequence

1. Invite-only beta for students, professionals, and job seekers with real document needs.
2. Publish supported-format quality benchmarks and privacy explanations.
3. Self-serve Pro plan after activation and retention are proven.
4. Team features and shared terminology after collaboration demand appears.
5. API and enterprise pilots only after operational reliability and security controls mature.

## Key business metrics

- Activation: first usable translated output.
- Weekly/monthly retained translators.
- Completion and repeat-use rates by format/language.
- Free-to-paid and trial-to-paid conversion.
- Gross margin and provider cost per completed unit.
- Expansion/contraction and churn by segment.
- Support tickets and correction effort per 100 jobs.
- Enterprise pipeline, sales cycle, implementation time, and net revenue retention.

## Commercial safeguards

- Plans are entitlement versions, so existing contracts remain reproducible.
- “Unlimited” is replaced by transparent included usage, concurrency, and fair-use terms.
- Provider price changes trigger margin alerts, not silent customer degradation.
- Enterprise security claims require evidence and contractual review.
- Billing records contain references and amounts, never card data.
- Refunds, failed payments, plan changes, prorations, taxes, and regional consumer rights are specified before launch.

## Validation questions

- Which format/language pairs create enough pain to pay for preservation?
- Is willingness to pay driven by pages, documents, characters, quality tier, or turnaround time?
- Which privacy controls are purchase drivers rather than checklist items?
- Does resume translation warrant a workflow/plan or act as acquisition?
- At what volume does private/self-hosted processing become economically viable?
